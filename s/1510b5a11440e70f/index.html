Better Pelicans

We ask AI models to draw a pelican riding a bicycle 🚲, and then we judge the results with entirely unreasonable rigor.

Experiment 0001 is complete — it produced a provisional canonical pelican, currently sitting in the gallery awaiting the owner's blessing. We make no claims about model training influence; we're scientists, not fortune tellers.

The benchmark

Since October 2024, a modest sentence has served as our informal probe of text-to-SVG models:

Generate an SVG of a pelican riding a bicycle.

It's an easy sentence and a brutally hard drawing: two distinct objects, a mechanically plausible interaction, correct depth ordering, and a valid, standalone, editable vector file. In short, we're asking for a small miracle in XML. The prompt was popularized by Simon Willison (blog, benchmark repository). See docs/benchmark.md for the canonical wording, variants and sources.

The experiment

Better Pelicans studies what it takes to go from that prompt to a genuinely good SVG: not just a valid file, but a recognizable pelican, a coherent bicycle, and an unambiguous act of riding. We have, on our own authority, the world's most meticulous standards for an activity nobody asked for.

Because the benchmark has been public since 2024, models may well have seen it — or its results — during training. In other words, the pelican may have peeked at the answers. That makes this prompt a useful lens on benchmark contamination, alongside composition, iterative repair and generalization. We measure what we can and disclose what we cannot — a humble amount of honesty for a project about birds.

Everything is reproducible: fixed prompts and settings, deterministic validation, local rendering, specialist critiques, and local repair rounds. Every artifact is hashed and logged. Future archaeologists will love us.

Methodology in brief

  1. Generate candidates with documented prompts and settings.
  2. Validate deterministically: XML, safety, size, complexity.
  3. Render at 256×256, 1024×1024 and transparent variants.
  4. Critique with specialist rubrics — anatomy, mechanics, interaction, composition, editability.
  5. Repair locally, at most three rounds per finalist. We believe in second chances, not fourth ones.
  6. Publish only what passes the publication checklist — only the very best pelicans get out.
